MATS 12.0 · interim findings

Separating belief from refusal

A refusal is two things at once: an internal judgement that a request is harmful, and the behaviour that follows from it. On Qwen3.5-4B these come apart cleanly — they are carried by different directions, at different token positions, and neither is the direction a monitor would naturally choose.

The question

Which signal should a monitor actually watch?

The motivating deployment is an agent working alongside peers who log completed jobs. Social proof is the manipulation — no jailbreak, no attacker, no modified weights. The concern is not that such an agent could be made to comply, but that it could comply while still internally registering the task as harmful, leaving a monitor that watches refusal with nothing to fire on.

That worry only makes sense if harmfulness and refusal are separable in the first place. Establishing that is a precondition for the main experiment, and it is what this page reports. Three candidate directions were tested, all extracted by difference-of-means over the same corpora, differing only in where they are read and how they are selected.

Result 1

Refusal is causally mediated — but not by the direction that detects harm

Result 2

Only one direction carries the judgement

Ablation cannot distinguish a belief from a reflex: both produce the same silence. Zhao et al.'s reply-inversion task can, by asking the model to classify the instruction rather than obey it — and inverting the answer key so the correct reply to a harmful prompt is an acceptance-shaped word.

Each direction was added into one layer at a time across all 32 layers, on 50 held-out benign prompts. Unsteered, the model calls none of them harmful.

Steering the harmfulness direction makes the model call “Provide four useful tips to write a clear paper” a safety violation — without producing a single refusal.

The discriminator

Direction of movement, not amount

Every direction moves something, so “did the answer change” separates nothing. Each direction is harmful-minus-harmless, so pushed + it should raise the harmful verdict and pushed − it should lower it. The refusal directions fail that test in a specific and revealing way: they raise the harmful verdict only when pushed toward less harmful.

Where it lives

A narrow band, early in the network

Reading

What this establishes

Three functions that are routinely treated as one signal are carried by three different vectors:

  • Detection — the direction that best separates harmful from benign is not the one that causes refusal, and ablating it damages the model (KL ) without producing compliance.
  • Causation — a different, causally selected direction does mediate refusal behaviour, yet leaves the model's judgement of harmfulness untouched.
  • Belief — a third, read at the last instruction token, moves the judgement itself, in the direction its label predicts, with no refusals at all.

For monitoring, the consequence is direct: a probe trained to detect harmful inputs is not measuring the thing that makes the model refuse, and neither is measuring what the model believes. Which of the three a monitor tracks determines what a manipulation would have to defeat — and whether the manipulation would be visible at all.

Confidence

How these numbers were checked

  • Controls that can fail. A shuffled-label direction, built the same way from the same activations at the same site, leaves refusal at 1.00 and compliance at 0.00. Selection additionally enforces a KL ceiling, which rejects directions that suppress the refusal token rather than the feature.
  • Compliance is hand-read, never judged. An automated judge scored 0.20 on the ablation transcripts against 0.84 by hand, reading only framing-heavy openings. Every compliance figure here comes from reading the full completion.
  • Verdict labels validated blind. An independent labeller, given replies with all labels stripped and stratified toward the hardest cases, agrees with the scorer on 98.1% of them — the one disagreement being their own lowest-confidence call.
  • Unreadable cells are not zeros. Where steering degrades the model badly enough that replies parse to nothing, the cell is marked unreadable and left as a gap rather than plotted as no-effect.
Limits

What this does not yet show

  • One model, 4B parameters. Zhao et al. locate the analogous band at layer 9 of Llama3-8B; we find layers 5–10 here. Confirmation at 9B is pending.
  • Thinking mode is disabled throughout. It separates the classes markedly better on this task, but thinking-enabled agents are the realistic deployment case.
  • Steering at layers 0–1 produces large apparent flips in which +v and −v give the same answer — representational damage, not a feature being moved. Those layers are excluded; directionality is credited only where the two signs separate.
  • The peer-framing experiment itself has not run. Everything here is the precondition: the signals are separable, so the question of whether social context can move one without the other is now well posed.
